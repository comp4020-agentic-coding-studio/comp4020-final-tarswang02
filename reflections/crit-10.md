# Crit 10 reflection — Proofroom

**The breakthrough.** What moved my work forward this week was learning to treat an agent's activity as a claim, not as progress. I asked a Claude Code session on the course key to do a visual redesign, and wrote into the prompt that it should keep improving only with evidence and never change things just to use up budget. It did the five things I asked for. Then it kept going for hours: more than two dozen commits of audit notes and test coverage I had not asked for, about $29 of the weekly budget in two hours, while its screen still looked busy and reasonable. I only saw the problem when I had the git log, the spend and the process list checked instead of reading the agent's own summary. Even my first diagnosis named the wrong session. I stopped it, confirmed the working tree was clean, and left its unrequested commits on a local branch, unmerged.

**What it changed about who I want to be as a developer.** A rule written in a prompt is not a stop condition. Next time I will give an agent a bounded task with an end I can check, such as one named outcome, a commit count or a time limit, and I will verify who is working and what changed before I trust a report. It also made me admit that I had been directing agents on a project I did not yet understand well enough to judge their output. That is what I want to fix before the final: Proofroom exists to keep claims and the evidence for them side by side, and I need to be able to do the same for my own work.
